Position sizing — margin, risk rule and Kelly

Two limits, and the smaller one wins: how many lots the margin can carry (within the margin cap above), and how many the risk rule allows before a stop-loss would cost more than you said you were willing to lose. Proof

Has any of this actually worked? The honest record, uncertainty included.

The weekly premium strategy was tested and it failed

Measured at the price a seller is actually paid, no horizon from 7 to 60 days clears t = 2. The apparent edge at 0 DTE was a σ error. The gate fired on 73% of 0 DTE observations and 0% at 3+ DTE, so it could only ever open where the model was broken: at 0 DTE it claimed 78.2% safety and delivered 57.9%. Drawdown reached 33.3% of cumulative profit.

What that σ error actually is, measured rather than assumed. This paragraph said the market prices same-day volatility "about a third higher" than India VIX. That was an estimate and it was too large. Across 39 readings on 14 sessions, at-the-money implied volatility runs 7% above India VIX once dead weekend days are taken out of the clock — 12% at the shortest horizons, not 33%. The raw comparison points the other way, and that is the part worth knowing: σ is spot × vol × √(dte/365) with calendar days, while India VIX is a thirty-day number whose per-day variance already averages in a normal weekend. A Friday-to-Tuesday weekly is half dead time, so on Fridays this site's σ is roughly 26% too large, and on Mondays about 12% too small. Every POP, EV, band and gate reading here is quietly weekday-dependent. Selecting σ from this expiry's own chain removes both errors, which is what ?sigma_source=chain_iv on the API does.

Everything below is kept because the measurements are worth reading, not because the signal is worth taking. The gate, the sizing and the Kelly figures on this site are not a validated trading system. The POP and EV columns in the option chain (Tools) inherit the same VIX-derived σ. Volatility smile — implied, both sides

Black–Scholes at zero drift, solved off the parity forward. Calls and puts at the same strike should sit on top of each other; where they separate, one side's quote is stale.

ATM straddle through the session

The price of the at-the-money straddle, minute by minute, from your own bars. This is what you are selling — when it falls through the day with the index still, that is theta arriving.

Chain

The same column you are reading in the table, drawn across strikes — calls solid, puts dashed, as on the smile. A table tells you the value at a strike; a curve tells you where the maximum is, which is the question you are usually actually asking.

Priced at the mid, never at close: a far strike that did not trade in the last minute carries a stale print. Quotes that are one-sided or absurdly wide are dropped and counted above rather than guessed at. Bars behind the rows are open interest, scaled to the largest on screen.

VWAP is volume-weighted for the whole session, from the pv the collector accumulates per bar — not an average of the minute closes, which would weight a minute that traded one lot the same as one that traded a thousand. A mid far below VWAP means the option has been sold down since most of its volume traded.

B/U reads price and open interest together, measured from this session's first bar. LB long buildup, price up and OI up — new buyers. SB short buildup, price down and OI up — new sellers. LU long unwinding, both down — holders closing. SC short covering, price up and OI down — writers buying back, which on a strike you have sold is the one worth noticing. Moves under 0.5% are shown as flat rather than dressed up as a signal.

Open interest by strike

Calls above the line, puts below. The tallest call bar is where the most writers have bet the index will not go — which is why it so often behaves like resistance. The tallest put bar is the mirror. Neither is a forecast; both are where the pain is concentrated.

Max pain — total loss to option writers

At each possible settlement price, what every open call and put would cost the people who wrote them. The minimum is max pain. It is a statistic about crowd positioning, not a prediction — but expiries do tend to gravitate toward it, which is why the distance is worth watching.

Per-strike buildup — ranked by OI change

Price and open interest moving together, per contract, from the session's first bar to the latest. Rising OI means new positions; falling OI means positions closed. The pairing names who was doing it. Read the agreement panel below before trusting the pattern — ranking by OI change sorts near-money strikes to the top, and that is where the index move drowns everything else.

Price, VWAP and moving averages

VWAP is each leg's own session volume-weighted average, summed — the average cost of assembling the structure leg by leg, not a price the combination itself ever traded at. Price above it means the structure costs more now than the session's average buyer paid.

RSI

On the structure's own price, not the index. A short straddle bleeding to theta drifts down all session, so a low reading here is often decay rather than weakness — which is exactly why the anchor matters.
